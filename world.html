The world and the journal

How the CircuitJourney scene boots, routes every frame, walks the traveller, opens journal pages, saves rule changes and builds the districts it walks on.

In 60 seconds

  • One MonoBehaviour runs the world. IsometricSlice, split over 19 partial files in Game/EmberGrove/Assets/Runtime/Exploration/, owns boot, input, walking, the camera, the HUD, the journal and the live Circuit state. Its only partner in the scene is IsometricSectorVisuals on the same Circuit world GameObject. §01
  • Boot is one coroutine. Start reads sector.json, loads the Circuit sidecars and the content pack, restores the save, builds the district window, then makes the camera, the world, the HUD and the title. A load error skips the remaining load steps and ends on an error panel. §02
  • Every frame goes through one router. Update gives the frame to the table lobby, a hosted session, the startup error or the world, in that order; inside the world it tries Start, Back, the open page and world input in turn. §03
  • Every rule change is ApplyCircuit(CircuitRules.X(...)). The toolkit returns a new state, ApplyCircuit adopts it and calls SaveCircuit, and a change that must be durable is rolled back if the write fails. §07
  • The world is authored first and procedural second. CircuitWorldLayout turns the run into a grid of up to three districts; IsometricSectorVisuals.Build binds the saved district and room prefabs onto it and draws procedural geometry only where nothing is bound. §08, §09

Before you start

Read the Codebase Tour first; this page assumes you know where the toolkit and the game live. The adventure rules themselves (CircuitRules, receipts, tasks) are on Rules Engine, the hosted battle a duel opens is on Hosted Sessions, the save file is on Saves & Settings and the menu toolkit is on UI & Menus. Class diagrams of the game assembly are generated on Diagrams. What the player sees in the book is owned by CIRCUIT_WAYBOOK.md; this page explains the code behind it.

01Where the pieces live

The world is the only build scene, Game/EmberGrove/Assets/Scenes/CircuitJourney.unity. Its Circuit world GameObject carries IsometricSlice and IsometricSectorVisuals. The scene also saves the prefab instances the visuals bind to; everything else the slice needs, it creates at runtime.

Saved in the scene

IsometricSlice: WorldPipeline, SurfaceMaterial and the opening-shot tuning fields.

IsometricSectorVisuals: the first district's fields, AdditionalDistricts (district 2), ReusableDistricts, StoryProps, CharacterAppearances and LodgeWaybook.

Prefab instances from Assets/ExplorationPrototype/: three ClubDistrict, six each of ClubRoomTable, ClubRoomVisit and ClubRoomCounter (the three role variants of the six flexible venues in districts 1 and 2), one LodgeWaybook, and the ClubTable, CourtyardPlanter and character-model instances placed in districts 1 and 2.

Made at runtime

World camera and the Late afternoon light (CreateCamera).

World interface canvas, and World input when no EventSystem exists (BuildHud).

Living station · replaceable prototype art, the generated root, and the traveller (IsometricSectorVisuals.Build, CreateTraveller).

IsometricCombatBridge, added to the slice's GameObject on first use, and the Hosted session child it makes for each duel, practice, lesson, Versus visit or Settings.

Update routing. The four outputs of UpdateFrameWorldInput are tried top to bottom and the first match returns; movement runs only when nothing else took the frame.

  • Guards. UpdateFrameWorld ignores input on the frame a page opened (modalFrame) and until blockedUntil, which CloseModal and a regained window focus set 0.12 s ahead. After a page closes, movement and Interact also wait for the stick to return to neutral (movementNeedsNeutral).
  • An open page. UpdateFrameModal turns a shoulder or page key into CycleCircuitPage, lets the west face button act on the task book (ActivateAdventureBookAction), and otherwise calls NavigateModal (in IsometricSlice.Hud.cs), which moves focus through MenuNavigationRows and submits the focused button. While the written title page is up, NavigateModal hands the direction to NavigateWaybook.
  • LateUpdate moves the camera (UpdateCamera), draws the wayfinder (UpdateWayfinder) and, while a page is open, keeps the world animating (visuals.AnimateWorld). It does nothing during a hosted session.

Which key does what is owned by PLAYER_CONTROLS.md § Keyboard and controller and, for the book, CIRCUIT_WAYBOOK.md § Moving around. The one Back behaviour the router implements is PLAYER_CONTROLS.md § One Back rule.

04Walking and pathing

Navigation is grid data: SectorDefinition.Rows holds one character per tile, and only '.' is walkable ('#' blocks, '~' is water). The toolkit's SectorNavigation answers every question about it; the slice only moves a transform.

MovementEntryHow it moves
Direct (keys, stick, d-pad)ReadMove → MoveScreenTurns the input into a camera-relative step at Sector.MoveSpeed. MoveWorld splits the step into pieces no longer than a tenth of a cell and tests each with CanStand (SectorNavigation.PointWalkable with a radius of 0.18 cell); a blocked step slides along X, then Z. Direct input cancels any route.
Routed (click, tap, Go)WalkTo(x, z) → FollowPathWalkTo needs a walkable target, then asks SectorNavigation.FindPath for a breadth-first, four-neighbour shortest path (ties broken east, north, west, south). FollowPath walks the tile centres through the same MoveWorld, and drops the route when a step is fully blocked.
  • Pointer. ReadPointer turns a mouse click, or a short tap, that is not over UI into GroundClick. A ray that hits a landmark (visuals.TryPickLandmark) routes to that landmark and remembers it as walkingDestination; otherwise the ray meets the ground plane and routes to that tile.
  • Guided travel. A task's Go sets adventureTravelTarget and calls ContinueAdventureTravel. A target outside the loaded window is reached through the arrival landmark (s<stage>-arrival) at the window's edge, and the walk resumes after each rebuild; dialogue never carries coordinates. The player-facing contract is CIRCUIT_WAYBOOK.md § One-click navigation.
  • District streaming. Each walking frame, UpdateCircuitWorld works out which district the traveller stands in (CircuitWorldLayout.StageAtLocalZ, capped at the highest open district). When that changes it sets worldFocusStage and calls RebuildCircuitPlace, which makes a new sector, rebuilds the visuals, recreates the traveller shifted by the district stride, keeps the camera where it was, saves, and resumes the walk.
  • Arrival. When a route ends near its walkingDestination (within 1.65 cells), UpdateCircuitWorldArrive refreshes the nearby landmark. Guided travel stops there and selects the location; a walk the player started by clicking a landmark goes straight on to CircuitInteract.

The rules a sector file must meet are the ExplorationChecks content contract; Tools/ExplorationChecks compiles the toolkit's Runtime/Exploration directly and exercises it outside Unity.

When it changes, the visuals show that landmark's nearby ring (SetNearby) and UpdatePrompt fills the Nearby action prompt with the landmark's title, CircuitNearbyHint and the CircuitNearbyAction button label. The prompt shows only while the world is ready, no page is open and the title is gone.

From a press to a journal page or a duel. Every arrow into a ShowCircuit box opens the journal (§06); every ApplyCircuit saves (§07). CircuitInteractAtLocation tries its branches in this order: a pending reward, an open event, an episode, a ready dialogue task, a Workshop, a Mentor, then the Location page.

The duel round trip

  1. Begin. PlayCircuitDuel applies CircuitRules.BeginDuel (or BeginEpisodeDuel for a friendly game, which must save). The run now holds a PendingDuel with a ReceiptId.
  2. Prepare. PlayCircuitDuelPrepare closes the journal, sets inEncounter, shows the loading cover, adds IsometricCombatBridge if needed, and sets its deck, seed, content root, kind and table (PrepareWorldTablePresentation).
  3. Play. BeginWorldTableSessionView moves the camera onto the table, then combat.Play(EncounterId, …, ReceiptId) hosts the battle until it resolves. The table hand-off is owned by CIRCUIT_UNIFIED_WORLD.md § The actual table; the hosted app is on Hosted Sessions.
  4. Commit early. While the session runs, UpdateFrameEncounter calls CommitFinishedCircuitDuel every frame. As soon as the hosted battle is over it applies ResolveDuel with the battle's outcome, so leaving on the result screen keeps the result. SaveProgress (pause, quit) tries the same commit first.
  5. Return. When Play returns, PlayCircuitDuel applies ResolveDuel again; a receipt already applied makes CircuitRules.ResolveDuel return an unchanged copy. EndWorldTableSessionView and PlayCircuitDuelReturn show the world again and open Ended, Reward or Location (or EpisodeResult/Episode for a friendly game).
Why the result is applied twice

The comment on CommitFinishedCircuitDuel gives the reason: the result is recorded while its screen is visible, "so exiting before Continue cannot discard earned cards". The later callback from the bridge can then arrive safely, because ResolveDuel remembers applied receipts and never grants twice. The check that combat.ReceiptId matches the pending duel stops a retained result from being applied to the next table.

06The journal: ShowCircuit, CircuitPage and DrawModal

Every journal window, the title included, is the one modal kind (Modal.Circuit) showing one CircuitPage. ShowCircuit(page, prepare) opens a page, a back stack remembers where the player came from, and DrawModal redraws the whole page from the current fields every time anything changes.

Opening a page

  1. ShowCircuitHistory. If the journal is closed the back stack is cleared. If it is open and the page changes, CaptureCircuitPage pushes a CircuitPageState of the page being left: its focused button, selected card, dialogue, event, episode, lesson position, builder search and filters, armed deck slot and target deck. The stack keeps at most 16 entries and drops the second-oldest, so the page the journal opened on is never lost.
  2. Reset and prepare. Per-page leftovers are cleared (builder status, an armed delete, a filter sheet), then the optional prepare action sets the new page's fields, for example OpenCircuitCard sets selectedCollectionCard.
  3. OpenModal(Modal.Circuit). Stops any walk, sets modalFrame, calls DrawModal and UpdatePrompt, and, outside the title, calls SaveProgress: opening any page writes the save.

Going back

BackCircuitPage first lets the page undo something inside itself (BackCircuitPageWithin: close Location tips, leave the Done pocket, stay on an episode result still being saved; BackCircuitPageNested: step back inside lessons, clear a builder pick, or let the title handle Back). Only then does it pop the stack, skipping Reward and Replace pages whose reward has been claimed. An empty stack closes the journal; otherwise RestoreCircuitPage restores the fields, falls back to Deck or Decks when the saved card or deck no longer exists, reopens the page and refocuses the button by name.

Drawing a page

DrawModal (in IsometricSlice.Hud.cs) clears modalButtons, calls DrawCircuitModal, applies the player's text size and contrast (ApplyWorldPreferenceText) and rebuilds focus links (RebuildModalNavigation). DrawCircuitModal in IsometricSlice.CircuitUi.cs:

  1. Sends the title and the club opening to their own surfaces (DrawCircuitModalOtherSurface); every other page hides the written title page.
  2. Sizes the book (DrawCircuitModalLayout: card pages are wider, a notice adds a band), builds the Circuit shade and Travel journal shell only if it is missing, and rebuilds the frame and page art, destroying the previous page's objects.
  3. Draws the heading and a Back button (hidden while an episode result is waiting to save), then the page itself through four switch methods.
  4. Draws the notice band, picks the default focus for the page, and starts the page-turn motion (TravellerPanelMotion).

07Every rule change goes through ApplyCircuit

The live adventure is the field circuit (a CircuitState). The slice never edits it: it calls a pure CircuitRules function, which returns a Result holding a new state or an error, and passes that result to ApplyCircuit. Outside ApplyCircuit, the only production code that assigns circuit is RestoreCircuit during boot.

flowchart TD
  R["CircuitRules.X(circuitDefinition, circuit, ...)<br/>returns a Result"] --> A["ApplyCircuit(result, requireSaved, restoreAtArrival)"]
  A --> OK{"result.IsSuccess?"}
  OK -->|no| NO["circuitNotice = the error message<br/>redraw the open page, return false"]
  OK -->|yes| SET["previous = circuit<br/>circuit = result.Value<br/>ApplyCircuitAdvanceTask"]
  SET --> SV["SaveCircuit(restoreAtArrival)<br/>CaptureCircuitSave, CircuitPersistence.Save"]
  SV --> W{"written?"}
  W -->|"yes, or the save was optional"| UP["CircuitObjective, MarkCircuitLandmarks,<br/>UpdatePrompt, return true"]
  W -->|"no, and requireSaved"| RB["circuit = previous<br/>Could not save notice, return false"]

ApplyCircuit in IsometricSlice.Circuit.cs. ApplyCircuitAdvanceTask selects the next task when the selected one has just become Done.

  • What SaveCircuit skips. It returns true without writing in the table lobby and when saves are suppressed (a verification run without a lifecycle folder), and false before the world is Ready. Otherwise SaveCircuitWrite captures a CircuitSave (version 5: the state, camera and motion settings, zoom, the traveller's position relative to the occupied district, and the onboarding and lesson fields) and hands its JSON to CircuitPersistence.Save. An IO failure becomes a notice.
  • Changes that must be durable pass requireSaved: true: starting an adventure (StartCircuitFromDeck, BeginNextAdventureWithCurrentDeck, which also pass restoreAtArrival), offering, beginning and committing an event, opening a story visit, and beginning or resolving a friendly episode duel (RetryEpisodeSave retries that one). Every other change, for example deck edits, SelectLocation, SelectTask, RecordDialogue or an ordinary duel, keeps the new state when the write fails and shows the notice.
  • Saves that are not rule changes. Fields that live in CircuitSave but not in CircuitState call SaveCircuit directly: the camera, motion and zoom buttons on the Controls page, the club badge, name, opening beat and welcome flag, and read lessons. ReturnToTitle and the return from Settings save too. SaveProgress runs on every page open except the title, on application pause and quit, and in OnDestroy; it commits a finished duel first.
  • Boot is the exception. RestoreCircuit assigns the restored state, a new profile, a new run, or the Interrupted resolution of a duel the process died in, before the world exists, so there is nothing to refresh and SaveCircuit would refuse anyway.
Rehearsals do bypass it. circuit is internal, and the CircuitSliceChecks partials in Assets/Tests/Runtime assign slice.circuit directly to stage fixtures, then call its redraw and rebuild members, such as RebuildCircuitPlace, directly. A state staged that way has not been saved or had its landmarks refreshed. See QA & Rehearsals.

What each CircuitRules function does, and how receipts make a repeated call safe, is on Rules Engine. File names, backups and version blocks are on Saves & Settings.

08CircuitWorldLayout: from a run to a sector

CircuitWorldLayout (in Game/EmberGrove/Assets/Runtime/Exploration/, with no Unity types) is a pure projection of the saved run onto a SectorDefinition. The slice calls it from MakeCircuitSector at boot and on every RebuildCircuitPlace. Tools/CircuitWorldChecks compiles the same file outside Unity.

ConstantValueMeaning
DistrictStride32Tile rows per district along Z.
Width29Tile columns of every district.
MaximumVisibleDistricts3The window: the district before the occupied one, the occupied one and the next.
  1. SelectTemplate picks one of the three Layouts in locations.json by the run seed, using the frozen template IDs circuit-courtyard-0 to -2, and rejects a template whose cell size is not 1.25 m. The template supplies cell size, speed, camera and colours.
  2. WindowFirstStage starts the window one district before the occupied one; the last district is the next one at most, which can be a locked preview from CircuitRules.PreviewDistrict.
  3. Build fills a grid of blocked tiles, paints garden pools, paints each visible location (Position: X = 5 + 9·node.X, Z = 4 + 6·node.Y within its district) and the roads between linked locations, and opens the seams between districts. That painted grid is kept as VisualRows.
  4. Each location becomes a LandmarkDefinition with its Id (s1-l1-1, s2-arrival, s1-tournament …), RoomId and RoomRevision, and a Kind such as cardtable, npc-<mentor>, workshop, reward or refuge. Locked locations become decorations. Each district border gets a gate-<stage>. The tile behind each venue is blocked, and a locked district's walkable tiles are closed.
  5. The result has the Id circuit-world-<seed>-<firstStage>, which IsometricSectorVisuals parses to find the window's first district. MakeCircuitSector then sets the title and objective, and the start tile to the current location when it lies in the focus district.

Coordinates in the sector are local to the window, so a long adventure never needs large numbers. CaptureCircuitSave stores the traveller relative to the occupied district (FocusStage), and RestoreWorldTraveller converts back. Which layout versions and room revisions are supported, and how old saves are kept on their geometry, is owned by MAINTAINING.md § Supported Circuit world layouts.

09IsometricSectorVisuals: authored first, procedural second

IsometricSectorVisuals.Build(sector) runs at boot and on every district rebuild. It first binds the saved prefab districts and rooms that the scene provides, then draws procedural geometry for every tile row and landmark that no saved prefab claims. In the shipping scene every visible district is bound; the procedural path remains the fallback.

10The traveller

The traveller is the player's figure in the world, the IsometricSlice.Traveller transform. The visuals create and animate it; only the slice moves it.

  • Creation. CreateTraveller(position) calls BuildCharacterTraveller, which spawns the player entry of CharacterAppearances under Traveller · replaceable character, or builds a procedural figure (BuildCraftedTraveller) when that entry has no model. Every RebuildCircuitPlace makes a new one and carries over its position (shifted by the district stride) and heading.
  • Animation. Each walking frame visuals.AnimateTraveller(velocity, time, reducedMotion) turns it toward its velocity, ticks its CharacterAnimation, and animates the rest of the world. A comment in IsometricSectorVisuals.Models.cs states the boundary: the world owns movement, and imported clips only pose the model.
  • Camera. UpdateCamera follows the traveller with a small lead in the direction of travel when FollowCamera is on, and otherwise frames the occupied district. Opening shots and the title camera take over first (TickOpeningWorldShot, UpdateTitleCamera).
  • Saved position. The position goes into CircuitSave relative to the occupied district (§08); during a table session the save uses the position the traveller will return to (WorldTableSavePosition).
  • Lobby travellers. The table lobby draws its members' travellers with CreateLobbyTraveller, AnimateLobbyTraveller and ReleaseLobbyTraveller, driven from IsometricSlice.Versus.cs.

The Traveller UI types

TravellerPictures is the picture vocabulary (the 21 values of TravellerPicture) shared by the world's menus, the written title page and the battle view. Its card-art resolver is static: LoadCircuit sets it with ConfigureArtwork and OnDestroy clears it. TravellerButtonMotion and TravellerPanelMotion (in TravellerUiMotion.cs) give buttons and page entrances their motion, and TravellerWaybookFrame is the book's frame mesh behind every journal page. The replacement contracts are in CIRCUIT_PRESENTATION.md § Rigged world characters in Unity and CIRCUIT_WAYBOOK.md § Motion and accessibility.
